The Windows test build now has a live dashboard, a studio that can move
while you are on, and a quieter desktop. It also fixes the microphone
dropouts that started in 0.9.103.

## Go Live

In Livestream → Upcoming, choose **Schedule on X** to plan a broadcast on
your X account: a title, description, thumbnail, start time, planned end,
and whether the replay stays available. When it is time, choose **Go Live…**
on the broadcast, pick your X destination, and start from Studio as usual.
A scheduled broadcast never starts on its own.

Upcoming gives YouTube and X their own sections. Public builds still cannot
connect a YouTube account while Google approval is pending. TikTok,
Instagram and YouTube Vertical stay available through their supported setup
paths.

## Chat and Orcle

Orcle is your live chat co-host. Write custom rules in plain language, see
richer flags when a message is a problem, and look for a Suggested badge on
comments worth putting on stream. You still decide. Orcle never posts
without you.

Comments from a native X broadcast land in the same studio feed as YouTube
and Twitch. Chat shows Twitch emotes, role badges, replies and first-time
chatters, with filters, Ctrl+F search and a **Send to** picker.

## Studio and layouts

Pick a different camera, microphone or display while you are recording,
streaming or both. Your recording keeps going and your stream stays
connected. Videorc shows the change as pending until the new source is
really in use, and tells you if a device could not switch so you can retry.
Switching to a different window during a session is not available on
Windows yet; choose a display instead, or change the window before you
start.

Save a complete scene (sources, layout, framing, background) and return to
it, including while recording. Each layout remembers its own framing.
Search the source picker, set your own layout shortcuts, and arrange the
canvas by hand: drag, resize, type exact sizes, or choose Freeform and
place sources anywhere.

## Stream Manager and remote

The Chat window is now the **Stream Manager**: your chat, your audience and
the health of your stream in one window. A row of stats shows how long you
have been live, how many people are watching (now including X), followers,
subs, tips, chat pace and whether each destination is healthy. Follows,
subs, gifts, cheers and raids get their own Activity feed. Turn on **Open
Stream Manager when I go live** in Settings to have it open by itself.

A phone on the same Wi-Fi becomes a chat monitor and a control deck. Scan a
QR code in Settings. There is nothing to install. Tap a message to put it on
stream, switch scenes, mute, or press and hold to stop.

## Reliability

Videorc can measure your computer and recommend a recording output it can
hold. Pages are flush panes instead of stacks of cards, with glass badges
and the Videorc icon in the title row. On Windows 11 version 22H2 or later,
the main window, the Stream Manager, Notes and Captions use the system Mica
material. Light mode now reaches every one of those windows, and switching
the theme restyles the ones already open.

Settings → About & updates now tells you when no Windows update is
published for you yet, and keeps checking in the background, so a newer
Alpha is found without restarting Videorc.

Since 0.9.103, a recording or stream could lose short pieces of microphone
audio, heard as clicks or a crackle. Microphone audio now gets a full
second of headroom, and Videorc keeps listening even while the file writer
is briefly held up. If audio is ever lost, the session log says how much
and why.

Windows remains an Alpha. See the [Windows Alpha notes](https://www.videorc.com/windows-alpha)
for supported hardware and current limitations.
