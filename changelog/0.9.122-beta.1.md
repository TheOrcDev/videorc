## Your microphone stays on when your Mac gets busy

Opening something heavy mid-stream, such as a site with its own live video
player, could make Videorc fall behind writing audio for a few seconds. After
that, it stopped using your microphone for the rest of the session, even
though the microphone was fine.

Videorc now keeps your microphone. At most, the moment it fell behind is
silent, and your voice is back as soon as it catches up. If a noticeable
stretch of audio drops, Videorc says so, and it tells you when the microphone
is back.

## System audio stops your own stream from echoing

If you watch your own stream on this Mac, for example in a Twitch tab, while
System audio is on, Videorc also captures that tab and sends it back into
your stream. Viewers then hear every word again a few seconds later.
Headphones don't prevent this: System audio records what apps play, not what
your speakers play.

Videorc now listens for your own voice coming back through System audio. When
it hears it, it pauses System audio, keeps your microphone going, and shows
**Paused** with a **Resume** button. Mute the tab, then press **Resume**. To
turn this off, use **Pause System audio if your stream echoes back** under
Sources → System audio.

## 4K and 1080p60 streaming on every plan

Live streaming quality is no longer a Premium feature. Every plan can stream
in 4K30 to YouTube and at 1080p60, within what each platform accepts.
Premium keeps cloud AI, live captions, Orcle and Noise Cleanup.
